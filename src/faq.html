Frequently asked questions

The questions people ask before they call.

If yours is not here, ask us. You do not have to give your name to ask a question.

Is it really free?

Yes. Counselling, legal guidance, safety planning and programme access cost you nothing. Donors cover it.

Will you tell my husband, my family or my employer?

No. Nothing is shared without your written consent. The only exception is where the law requires us to report a child at risk of serious harm — and we will tell you if that situation arises.

Do I have to report to the police?

No. Many women choose not to, and that is your decision. We will explain what reporting involves so you can decide with full information, and we will support you either way.

Do I have to leave my husband?

No. We do not pressure anyone to leave. Many women come to us while still in the relationship, and we help them stay as safe as possible.

What if I go back to him?

Then we are still here. This is normal and it is not a failure. Come back whenever you are ready.

I’m not sure it’s bad enough to count.

If you are asking that question, you are welcome here. There is no minimum threshold.

Do you help men?

Our programmes are designed for women, which is where our clinical expertise and safeguarding structures lie. Men experiencing abuse can contact us and we will refer you to organisations equipped to help.

Can I volunteer if I’m a survivor myself?

Yes, and many of our team are. We ask that you be at a stable point in your own recovery, and we will discuss this openly and supportively during screening.

[Locations]. Phone and WhatsApp support is available anywhere in [country].

Can someone see that I visited this website?

Possibly. A browser keeps a history of the pages you open, and a shared account or a monitored phone can show more than that.

If you are in immediate danger, stop reading and call emergency services. If you cannot speak safely, send us a message and we will follow your lead on how and when to respond.
