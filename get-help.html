Get help

You don’t need proof. You don’t need a plan. You just need to start.

You do not have to call it abuse. You do not have to be sure. You do not have to have decided anything. If something in your relationship, your home or your workplace is making you afraid, you can talk to us.

Everything you tell us is confidential. We will not contact anyone without your permission. There is no cost.

If you are in immediate danger, stop reading and call emergency services on [emergency number]. If you cannot speak safely, send us a message and we will follow your lead on how and when to respond.

What happens when you contact us

Four steps. No forms first.

1

You talk. We listen.

No interrogation, no forms first. You share what you want to share.

2

We tell you your options.

All of them, honestly, including the ones that are difficult.

3

You choose.

We do not decide anything for you.

4

We stay.

Whatever you choose, we follow up — and if you change your mind later, the door is open.

Is this abuse?

Abuse is not only physical. If any of these are familiar, you are welcome here:

  • Physical — hitting, choking, restraining, throwing objects, harm to your children or pets.
  • Emotional and psychological — constant criticism, humiliation, threats, silent treatment as punishment, being told you are mad or imagining things.
  • Sexual — any sexual act you did not freely agree to, including within marriage.
  • Financial — controlling your money, preventing you from working, taking your earnings, keeping you in debt.
  • Digital — monitoring your phone, tracking your location, demanding passwords, sharing your images without consent.
  • Isolation — being cut off from family, friends, church, work or medical care.
  • Spiritual — using scripture, faith or a religious leader’s authority to justify control or to force you to stay.

You do not need to tick every box. One is enough.

Safety planning

A safety plan is not a decision to leave. It is a set of options ready in case you need them.

  • Identify one person outside the home who knows your situation.
  • Know which room in the house has an exit and no weapons; avoid kitchens and bathrooms during conflict.
  • Keep a small bag of essentials somewhere safe — with a neighbour, at work, at church.
  • Photograph and store key documents in a private cloud account he does not know about: ID, birth certificates, marriage certificate, property papers, medical records.
  • Agree a code word with someone you trust that means “call for help”.
  • Memorise two phone numbers in case your phone is taken.
  • If you have children, agree on where they go and who they call.

Our team will work through a full, personalised plan with you. This list is a starting point only.

For friends and family

Say

“I believe you.”
“This is not your fault.”
“I’m here whether you stay or go.”

Don’t say

“Why don’t you just leave?”
“Are you sure it was that bad?”
“Think about the children.”

Do not confront the abuser. Do not issue ultimatums. Do not withdraw when she does not do what you want. Isolation is the abuser’s most effective tool — your steady presence is the counterweight.

Call us. You can get guidance on supporting her without breaching her trust.

Finding a shelter

We do not publish shelter addresses. Publishing them puts every woman inside at risk.

What we do instead: we refer you to a partner facility we have inspected ourselves, we confirm there is space before you travel, and we arrange transport where we can.

To start that process, call or message the helpline. You do not need a referral from anyone.

What this costs you

Nothing. Counselling, legal guidance, safety planning and programme access are free. Donors cover the cost.

Nobody at Strandfold will ever ask you for money, and nobody will ask you to tell your story publicly in exchange for help. If anyone claiming to represent us does, that is not us — please tell us.

Your safety

Covering your tracks

A browser keeps a record of the pages you visit. If someone else can use your phone or computer, here is how to reduce what they can see.

  1. Use private browsing next time. Chrome: menu → New Incognito tab. Safari: tabs → Private. Firefox: menu → New private window. Pages opened this way are not added to your history.
  2. Clear your history now. Chrome: menu → History → Clear browsing data → choose Browsing history and Cookies. Safari on iPhone: Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data.
  3. Delete only some entries if clearing everything would look strange. In the history list you can remove single pages and leave the rest.
  4. Check what else is watching. Location sharing, shared cloud accounts, a linked tablet, or a family plan can show your activity even after you clear history.
  5. Safest of all: use a phone or computer he has never had access to — a friend's, a library's, or one at work.

Quick exit: the red button at the top of every page leaves this site straight away and removes this page from the Back button. You can also press the Esc key twice quickly.

Clearing history is not a guarantee. If you believe your phone is being monitored, contact us from a different device.
